Hospital pilot model

A focused six-month pilot built around one care-transition workflow.

AfterNest is designed to begin with one hospital partner, one defined discharge population, and one shared operating model. The goal is to test whether a supervised non-clinical execution layer can reliably resolve practical barriers without duplicating clinical care.

Operating model

Clear ownership on both sides of the handoff.

The hospital remains responsible for clinical care and escalation. AfterNest owns only partner-approved practical support tasks after patient opt-in.

Pilot approval gates

What must be settled before the first real referral.

A hospital-ready pilot requires more than a good concept. These workstreams would need partner review and formal approval before live patient use.

1 · Clinical workflow

Referral + escalation

Eligibility, exclusions, warm handoff, contact expectations, after-hours limitations, and escalation destination.

2 · Privacy + legal

Data + consent

Minimum-necessary information, patient authorization, agreements, retention, access controls, insurance, and regulatory review.

3 · Workforce

Training + supervision

Screening, background requirements, scope boundaries, language support, supervision ratios, quality checks, and incident response.

4 · Measurement

Scorecard + governance

Baseline definitions, reporting cadence, patient experience measures, cost capture, issue review, and go/no-go criteria.

Proposed pilot scope

Small enough to control. Structured enough to learn.

The academic model assumes a single-site or tightly bounded service-line launch so the team can learn the workflow before considering expansion.

Population

Patients discharged home without reliable support

Initial eligibility could focus on older adults, people living alone, or patients whose recovery plan depends on support that may not be available at home.

Duration

Approximately 30 days per patient

The goal is resolution-based graduation, not indefinite case management. Open items either close, escalate, or hand off to sustainable support.

Launch window

Six-month operating pilot

Includes readiness, soft launch, supervised delivery, monthly scorecards, a midpoint review, and a formal closeout recommendation.

Pilot scorecard

Measure execution before claiming clinical impact.

The hospital should be able to see whether the operating model works without confusing process completion with readmission reduction or other clinical outcomes.

Funding framework

Fund a defined pilot, not an undefined promise.

The proposed nonprofit model combines partner support, grants, philanthropy, community sponsorship, university collaboration, and individual giving. Patients would not be charged for the pilot service.

01 · READINESS

Build safely

Governance, legal review, insurance, training, policies, referral design, technology, and evaluation planning.

02 · DELIVERY

Operate reliably

Program supervision, Navigator capacity, resource coordination, multilingual support, quality review, and partner communication.

03 · EVIDENCE

Learn from the pilot

Operational reporting, patient and staff experience, resolution tracking, cost analysis, midpoint review, and closeout recommendations.

Clinical and organizational boundary

Built to complement existing teams.

AfterNest should not replace transitional care, nursing, social work, case management, qualified language services, home health, or community organizations. The model is valuable only if it reduces practical execution burden while preserving clear clinical ownership.

Partner discussion

Start with the workflow, not the technology.
