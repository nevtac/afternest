Hospitals & sponsors

A focused pilot before broad expansion.

AfterNest is designed to launch with one anchor hospital partner in San Francisco, validate the referral and Navigator workflow, and use pilot results to inform future funding and expansion.

Proposed partner

UCSF Health is the team’s preferred potential pilot partner. No partnership or sponsorship is currently confirmed.

Hospital partnership model

Clear ownership on both sides of the handoff.

The hospital retains clinical responsibility. AfterNest takes ownership of approved practical support tasks after the patient opts in.

Hospital partner

Identify, refer, escalate.

Identify an appropriate patient before discharge
Explain the optional AfterNest program
Support approved referral and consent workflow
Define clinical escalation contact and boundaries
Participate in pilot review and workflow refinement
AfterNest

Match, coordinate, follow through.

Assign a trained Recovery Navigator
Create a Non-Clinical Recovery Support Plan
Coordinate transportation, technology, language, caregiver, and resource needs
Track practical tasks to resolution, escalation, decline, or handoff
Report aggregate pilot process and outcome measures
Funding case

Fund the execution layer around recovery.

A diversified nonprofit model can combine grants, philanthropy, community sponsorships, hospital support, and individual giving without charging the vulnerable patient for the pilot service.

01 · STARTUP

Build the pilot

Governance, legal review, insurance, Navigator training, referral workflow, operating procedures, technology, and evaluation design.

02 · DELIVERY

Support patients

Navigator supervision, transportation coordination, resource-navigation capacity, multilingual recruitment, and program operations.

03 · EVIDENCE

Measure what works

Track enrollment, contact, practical barriers, resolution status, escalation, patient experience, follow-up logistics, and program cost.

Partner evidence

Measure the execution, not just activity.

AfterNest would distinguish between a resource being offered and a practical barrier actually moving toward resolution. That allows a partner to see what happened after referral without confusing process measures with clinical outcomes.
