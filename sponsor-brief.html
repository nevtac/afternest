Sponsor brief
AfterNest · Academic Prototype

Support the practical side of recovery.

AfterNest is a proposed nonprofit recovery-support pilot for vulnerable patients leaving the hospital without enough practical help at home. A trained Recovery Navigator would help patients work through non-clinical barriers for approximately the first 30 days after discharge.

The model

One trained Recovery Navigator. One personalized Non-Clinical Recovery Support Plan. Practical barriers move toward a clear outcome: resolved, handed off, declined, or escalated.

Why support is needed

The pilot depends on more than volunteer goodwill. A responsible program requires nonprofit formation, leadership, screening, training, supervision, hospital integration, transportation and community-resource coordination, technology, privacy protections, and evaluation.

Funding structure in the final proposal

The paper identifies grants, community foundations, hospital partnerships, fundraising, sponsorships, and individual donations as potential outside funding sources. The presentation similarly emphasizes diversified funding so the organization is not dependent on one hospital or one donor.

Potential funding and relationship targets

The project materials identify the Saint Francis Foundation and San Francisco Public Health Foundation as possible grant targets, along with the UCSF Health donor network, Bronco Alumni Network, community sponsors, and individual donors as potential relationship sources. None of these organizations or networks is represented as a confirmed sponsor or funder.

What support enables

  • Nonprofit formation and governance
  • Hospital partnership development
  • Recovery Navigator recruitment, screening, training, and supervision
  • Transportation and community-resource coordination
  • Technology and administrative workflows
  • Pilot measurement, feedback, and SOP refinement

What success looks like after six months

A partner-reviewed pilot showing whether AfterNest can recruit and train Navigators, integrate into one hospital discharge workflow, support a limited patient group, move practical barriers toward resolution, gather patient and healthcare worker feedback, and identify the changes required before any larger launch.

Academic planning document only. No sponsorship, 501(c)(3) status, hospital partnership, or audited budget currently exists.
