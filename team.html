Governance & leadership

A hospital-facing program needs accountable leadership before it needs scale.

The academic project team developed the current AfterNest operating model. A real nonprofit pilot would require a separate board and leadership structure with healthcare, operations, finance, legal/privacy, workforce, and community-partnership expertise.

Proposed governance model

Roles that would need to exist before a real pilot launches.

The model is role-first by design. The organization should recruit people against explicit accountability profiles rather than assigning titles simply to make the nonprofit appear complete.

Governance

Board of Directors

Independent oversight of mission, risk, finances, executive performance, conflicts, and major partner commitments.

  • Healthcare/community perspective
  • Business or nonprofit governance
  • Finance, legal, or fundraising capability
Executive accountability

Executive Director

Owns strategy, partner relationships, fundraising, board reporting, staffing, and overall program accountability.

  • Healthcare or nonprofit leadership
  • Cross-sector partnership development
  • Operational decision-making
Clinical interface

Medical Social Work Advisor

Advises referral boundaries, community-resource coordination, escalation design, and patient-support standards.

  • Hospital transition experience
  • Community-resource expertise
  • Clinical boundary guidance
Operations

Director of Operations

Owns SOPs, referral intake, Navigator workflow, quality review, staffing capacity, and day-to-day pilot execution.

  • Program operations
  • Process and quality design
  • Cross-functional coordination
Workforce

Navigator Program Manager

Recruits, screens, trains, schedules, supervises, coaches, and evaluates Recovery Navigators.

  • Volunteer/workforce management
  • Training and coaching
  • Escalation discipline
Risk & compliance

Legal / Privacy Lead

Reviews consent, information-sharing, insurance, scope, transportation, privacy, contracts, and regulatory requirements.

  • Healthcare privacy experience
  • California nonprofit knowledge
  • Risk management
Finance & development

Finance / Development Lead

Builds budgets, internal controls, grant strategy, donor reporting, sustainability planning, and diversified funding.

  • Nonprofit finance
  • Grant development
  • Fundraising operations
Partnerships

Hospital & Community Partnerships Lead

Maintains hospital, university, community-resource, sponsor, and workforce-development relationships.

  • Partner stewardship
  • Community engagement
  • Clear external communication
Systems

Product / Systems Lead

Maintains referral, Navigator, resource, reporting, access-control, and audit systems with a privacy-by-design posture.

  • Secure workflow design
  • Accessibility
  • Data minimization
Board composition

Collective capability matters more than impressive titles.

A responsible board would need enough independence and experience to challenge the operating team, protect patients and mission, and make disciplined go/no-go decisions about the pilot.

Healthcare

Transitions + community health

Understands hospital discharge, social needs, care coordination, and where non-clinical support can create risk or value.

Risk

Legal + privacy

Can scrutinize consent, data-sharing, volunteer scope, insurance, transport, and partner contracts.

Stewardship

Finance + nonprofit governance

Brings budget discipline, internal controls, fundraising oversight, and fiduciary accountability.

Lived perspective

Patient + caregiver voice

Ensures the service is designed around the people expected to use it, not only around organizational convenience.

Leadership standard

The pilot should be supervised like a real service, even while it is still being tested.

That means clear accountability, written role boundaries, documented incident and escalation processes, workforce supervision, routine performance review, and board-level visibility into meaningful risk.
