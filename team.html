Future nonprofit leadership

The roles needed before a real pilot launches

Instead of assigning impressive titles early, AfterNest would recruit for the capabilities required to protect patients, supervise volunteers, manage funding, and work responsibly with a hospital partner.

Governance

Board of Directors

Independent oversight of mission, risk, finances, executive performance, conflicts, and major commitments.

Executive leadership

Executive Director

Owns strategy, partnerships, fundraising, staffing, board reporting, and overall program accountability.

Clinical interface

Medical Social Work Advisor

Advises referral boundaries, resource coordination, escalation design, and patient-support standards.

Operations

Director of Operations

Owns SOPs, referral intake, quality review, staffing capacity, and day-to-day pilot execution.

Volunteer workforce

Navigator Program Manager

Recruits, screens, trains, schedules, supervises, coaches, and evaluates Recovery Navigators.

Risk & stewardship

Legal, Privacy & Finance Support

Reviews consent, information-sharing, insurance, scope, contracts, controls, grants, and nonprofit compliance.

Board composition

A balanced board should be able to challenge the program, not just champion it.

Collective capability would matter more than prestige. The board should bring enough independence and relevant experience to make disciplined decisions about safety, sustainability, and whether the pilot is ready to proceed.

Healthcare

Transitions + community health

Understands discharge, social needs, care coordination, and the boundary between clinical and non-clinical support.

Risk

Legal + privacy

Can scrutinize consent, data-sharing, volunteer scope, insurance, transport, and partner agreements.

Stewardship

Finance + nonprofit governance

Brings budget discipline, internal controls, fundraising oversight, and fiduciary accountability.

Lived perspective

Patient + caregiver voice

Keeps the service grounded in the people expected to use it rather than only organizational convenience.
