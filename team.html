Our team

An academic project team designing a proposed nonprofit service.

The current AfterNest concept was developed by a Santa Clara University MBA project team. The final project plan also defines the leadership roles that would need to be recruited before a real nonprofit pilot launches.

Important distinction

The people below are the academic project team. The nonprofit leadership roles shown later on this page are proposed future positions, not current appointments.

Proposed nonprofit leadership

The roles listed in the final project plan

The Build-Up Phase calls for a leadership and administrative team that can launch the organization, establish operating procedures, recruit and train volunteers, manage funding, and keep the service compliant.

Governance

Board of Directors

Provides nonprofit oversight and supports major governance, funding, and strategic decisions.

Executive leadership

Executive Director

Oversees the program with the Board and carries overall accountability for the organization.

Program leadership

Program Director

Coordinates the AfterNest program, project management, growth, and future expansion.

Operations

Director of Operations

Develops standard operating procedures and oversees business processes and operating workflows.

Volunteer workforce

Volunteer Manager

Recruits, trains, schedules, and manages the volunteer Recovery Navigator workforce.

Finance

Director of Finance

Manages resource allocation, budgets, fundraising needs, and financial planning.

Communications

Director of Communications

Leads marketing, outreach, and messaging for AfterNest services and partnerships.

People

Head of Human Resources

Manages staffing and personnel processes for the growing nonprofit organization.

Legal

Head of Legal

Helps ensure the nonprofit and pilot comply with applicable local, state, federal, privacy, and healthcare requirements.

Recovery Navigator recruiting

The volunteer pipeline described in the paper

The project plan proposes recruiting volunteers from groups that may value hands-on service experience while also bringing useful familiarity with health and community settings.

Medical students

Seasonal and hands-on volunteering

Medical school students in the area are identified as one potential source of Recovery Navigators.

Public health students

Community-health experience

Public health students are another proposed recruiting pool for internships and volunteer roles.

Retired healthcare workers

Longer-term support

Retired healthcare workers are identified as potential longer-term volunteers or staff members.
