The gap we are addressing

A reminder is useful. It is not the same as support.

Some patients know exactly what they are supposed to do next and still cannot do it. The barrier may be a ride, a portal, a language gap, a missing caregiver, or simply not knowing how to reach the resource they were given.

Plan

The hospital establishes the medical discharge plan and clinical follow-up.

Barrier

Real-life constraints can make the next step difficult to complete once the patient is home.

Follow-through

AfterNest assigns a trained person to help resolve the practical barrier and route clinical questions back to care.

The first three barriers

Transportation. Technology. Language navigation.

Our pilot starts with a deliberately focused framework, then adds practical support around the specific needs identified during referral.

01 · TRANSPORTATION

Can the patient actually get there?

Identify an appropriate ride option, help coordinate logistics, and follow through until transportation is arranged or the barrier is handed off.

02 · TECHNOLOGY

Can the patient use the tools?

Help with portals, video visits, digital scheduling, device basics, and non-app alternatives for patients who are not comfortable online.

03 · LANGUAGE

Can the patient navigate the process?

Provide non-clinical support in the patient’s preferred language while leaving medical interpretation to qualified healthcare language services.

From referral to resolution

A visible recovery workflow, not another disconnected check-in.

01

Identify

A hospital team member recognizes a patient who may benefit from practical support.

02

Opt in

The patient chooses to participate and completes the limited referral and consent workflow.

03

Plan

AfterNest creates a personalized Non-Clinical Recovery Support Plan around identified barriers.

04

Resolve

A trained Recovery Navigator owns practical tasks through resolution, escalation, or handoff.

05

Graduate

The patient completes support when priority barriers are resolved or sustainable resources are in place.

Who the pilot is for

Start with the patients most likely to need another layer of support.

Living situation

People living alone

Patients who return home without a reliable person nearby to help with practical recovery tasks.

Caregiver access

Limited nearby support

Patients whose family or caregivers are unavailable, distant, working, or unable to take on all follow-up logistics.

Language access

First language is not English

Patients who may benefit from non-clinical navigation and resource support in their preferred language.

Age & access

Older or digitally excluded

Patients who may face added transportation, technology, or resource-access challenges after discharge.

A deliberate boundary

AfterNest knows the barrier, not the medicine.

The pilot is intentionally designed around practical recovery support. Medical care remains with the patient’s licensed healthcare team.

AfterNest can help with

Transportation coordination and appointment logistics
Technology and digital-literacy support
Non-clinical language navigation
Caregiver and basic-needs coordination
Community resource follow-through
Organizing questions for the healthcare team

AfterNest does not provide

Diagnosis, treatment, medication, or symptom advice
A replacement for nurses, physicians, social workers, or home health
Unrestricted medical-record access for Navigators
Unqualified medical interpretation
Clinical decision-making or emergency response
A guarantee of clinical outcomes or no readmission
