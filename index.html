Hospital to home

The handoff should not end at the hospital door.

AfterNest is built around a simple idea: some discharge plans fail in the real world because the patient lacks practical support, not because the clinical plan was unclear. The program adds a trained person who can stay with those practical tasks until there is a defined outcome.

01
Hospital defines the medical planClinical responsibility, treatment decisions, and escalation remain with licensed care.
02
AfterNest receives the practical handoffOnly approved non-clinical barriers move into the Recovery Support Plan.
03

The operating gap

A discharge plan can be clinically sound and still be difficult to execute.

Existing care-transition teams may already provide calls, education, social work, and follow-up coordination. AfterNest is designed to sit underneath that work and take ownership of approved non-clinical barriers that otherwise remain unresolved after the patient gets home.

Plan

The hospital defines the clinical discharge plan, follow-up, and escalation pathway.

Barrier

The patient may still lack a ride, digital access, language navigation, caregiver support, or a workable community-resource connection.

Execution

AfterNest gives those practical tasks a named owner and follows them until they are resolved, declined, escalated, or handed off.

Focused pilot scope

Three practical barriers. One accountable support layer.

The pilot starts with transportation, technology, and language navigation because each can make an otherwise appropriate next step much harder to complete. Caregiver and basic-needs coordination can be added when identified through referral.

Integration model

Designed to extend the care-transition workflow, not duplicate it.

The hospital retains clinical ownership. AfterNest owns approved practical follow-through after patient opt-in.

Recovery Navigator

More than a reminder. A person who stays with the problem.

The Navigator is not a clinician and does not interpret the medical plan. Their value is persistence: help coordinate the practical next step, verify whether it worked, and know when to hand the issue back to the healthcare team.

1
CoordinateTransportation, technology, language navigation, caregiver support, and approved resources.
2
VerifyDo not stop at “resource provided.” Confirm whether the practical barrier actually moved forward.
3
EscalateClinical questions, symptoms, medications, and treatment concerns go back to the approved care-team pathway.
Patient lifecycle

A defined pathway from referral to responsible graduation.

01 · PRE-DISCHARGE

Refer

Hospital team identifies an eligible patient and introduces the optional program.

02 · OPT-IN

Assess

Recovery Readiness Check captures only the practical information needed for support.

03 · 0-2 DAYS

Assign

A trained Navigator confirms communication preferences and priority barriers.

04 · WEEKS 1-4

Resolve

Tasks move toward resolution, escalation, decline, or formal handoff.

05 · CLOSE

Graduate

Support closes when priority practical barriers are resolved or sustainable support is in place.

Partner readiness

What would need to be true before a real hospital pilot.

The prototype is intentionally more conservative than a consumer app. A real launch would begin only after the operating, privacy, escalation, supervision, and reporting structure is approved with the partner.

Clinical integration

Named escalation pathway

Clear routing for clinical questions and defined partner contacts.

Privacy & legal

Approved data model

Minimum-necessary information, consent, retention, access, and partner agreements.

Workforce

Screened and supervised Navigators

Training, role boundaries, quality review, and escalation discipline.

Evidence

Predefined scorecard

Process, resolution, experience, feasibility, and cost measures agreed before launch.

Clinical boundary

AfterNest owns the practical barrier, not the medicine.

Medical care remains with the licensed healthcare team. The proposed service does not diagnose, treat, advise on medications, interpret symptoms, replace home health, or promise clinical outcomes.

AfterNest can support

Transportation and appointment logistics
Portal, video-visit, and digital-access support
Non-clinical preferred-language navigation
Caregiver and basic-needs coordination
Community-resource follow-through
Organizing questions for the healthcare team

AfterNest does not provide

Diagnosis, treatment, medication, or symptom advice
A replacement for nursing, social work, physicians, or home health
Unrestricted medical-record access
Unqualified clinical interpretation
Emergency response or clinical decision-making
A guarantee of reduced readmission or other clinical outcomes
