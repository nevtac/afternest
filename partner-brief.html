AfterNest · Proposed San Francisco Pilot · For Partner Discussion

Hospital Partner Brief

AfterNest is a proposed nonprofit, non-clinical recovery execution program for patients whose discharge plan is in place but whose ability to complete the next practical steps at home may be limited by transportation, technology, language navigation, caregiver availability, or basic-needs barriers.

The operating proposition

Give each approved practical barrier a named owner, next action, visible status, and defined close state. The hospital retains clinical responsibility. AfterNest takes responsibility only for the non-clinical execution work agreed in the pilot design.

Why this is different from reminders or referral lists

The model is organized around practical task ownership and verified closure. A referral is not considered complete simply because a phone number or resource was provided. The Navigator follows the task until it is resolved, declined, escalated, or formally handed off to another accountable party.

Proposed pilot population

A narrow group of patients discharged home without reliable practical support. The starting population could include older adults, people living alone, patients without a nearby caregiver, or another partner-selected cohort with a clear concentration of transportation, technology, language-navigation, or resource-access barriers.

Patient workflow

  1. Identify before discharge. An approved hospital team member identifies an eligible patient.
  2. Patient opts in. The optional program and information-sharing boundaries are explained through a partner-approved process.
  3. Recovery Readiness Check. The referral captures only practical support needs required for the program.
  4. Navigator assignment. One trained Recovery Navigator owns the practical Recovery Support Plan.
  5. Resolve and escalate. Tasks move toward resolution while clinical questions return to the approved care-team pathway.
  6. Graduate responsibly. Priority barriers have a confirmed outcome or sustainable next owner before the case closes.

Division of responsibility

Hospital partner

Define eligibility and exclusions, introduce the optional program, approve referral and consent workflow, establish clinical escalation contacts, review privacy and operational design, and participate in pilot governance.

AfterNest

Recruit and supervise Navigators, create practical Recovery Support Plans, coordinate approved support tasks, maintain non-clinical scope, document task status, escalate appropriately, and produce the agreed pilot scorecard.

What AfterNest would not do

  • Diagnose, treat, advise on medications, or interpret symptoms
  • Replace nursing, physicians, social work, case management, home health, or qualified healthcare interpreters
  • Provide unrestricted EHR access to Navigators
  • Represent a practical process metric as proof of a clinical outcome
  • Guarantee lower readmission, fewer complications, or any other clinical result

Six-month pilot questions

  • Can the program reach the intended patients at the right point in the discharge workflow?
  • Do practical barriers move toward resolution faster and more consistently?
  • Can Navigators stay inside the approved non-clinical role and escalate reliably?
  • Does the model fit hospital workflow without creating excessive administrative burden?
  • Is the workforce and funding model feasible enough to justify a larger test?

Readiness gates before launch

Clinical workflow and escalation, privacy/legal review, consent and information-sharing design, workforce screening and supervision, insurance/risk review, transportation policy, language-access boundaries, resource standards, data access/retention, incident response, scorecard definitions, and pilot governance must be approved before real patient use.

Recommended next step

Conduct a 60-90 minute workflow-design session with care transitions, social work, nursing, operations, privacy/compliance, and an appropriate clinical sponsor. Map the current discharge process, identify where non-clinical tasks remain unresolved, and determine whether a narrow AfterNest pilot would add capacity without duplicating existing services.

Academic prototype for partner discussion. UCSF Health has been considered as a proposed target partner in the project planning process; no partnership, endorsement, sponsorship, or clinical integration is confirmed. Real implementation would require partner, legal, privacy, insurance, clinical, and regulatory review.
