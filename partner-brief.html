AfterNest · Proposed Hospital Partnership · Academic Prototype

Hospital Partner Brief

AfterNest is a proposed nonprofit, non-clinical recovery support program designed for patients who may leave the hospital without enough practical support to carry out the next steps of recovery at home.

The operating gap

A discharge plan can be clinically appropriate and still be difficult to execute if the patient lacks transportation, cannot use the required technology, has limited English proficiency, has no reliable caregiver nearby, or cannot access the resources they were given.

Who the pilot would serve

The initial pilot would focus on a limited group of patients discharged home who face one or more practical support barriers, especially older adults, people living alone, people without reliable nearby caregivers, and patients facing transportation, technology, language-navigation, or basic-needs challenges.

What AfterNest would provide

  • Pre-discharge opt-in and limited non-clinical referral workflow
  • Assignment to a trained Recovery Navigator
  • Personalized Non-Clinical Recovery Support Plan
  • Transportation coordination and appointment logistics
  • Technology and digital-literacy support
  • Non-clinical language navigation in the patient’s preferred language when available
  • Caregiver and community-resource coordination
  • Practical task tracking through resolution, escalation, decline, or handoff

What AfterNest would not provide

  • Diagnosis, treatment, medication, or symptom advice
  • Unqualified clinical interpretation
  • Home health or licensed clinical services
  • Unrestricted EHR access for Recovery Navigators
  • A guarantee that the patient will not experience complications or readmission

Proposed division of responsibility

Hospital partner

Identify appropriate patients, explain the optional program, establish the approved referral/consent process, define clinical escalation contacts, and participate in pilot review.

AfterNest

Recruit and train Navigators, create practical Recovery Support Plans, coordinate approved support, supervise the Navigator workforce, track non-clinical task outcomes, and report aggregate pilot measures.

Six-month pilot deliverable

A focused pilot at one hospital location in which enrolled patients receive approximately 30 days of Recovery Navigator support. The pilot would test referral, consent, training, supervision, barrier-resolution workflows, privacy boundaries, escalation procedures, resource availability, patient experience, and operating feasibility.

What the pilot would measure

  • Referral and enrollment volume
  • First-contact timeliness
  • Navigator match rate
  • Barriers identified by category
  • Practical tasks resolved, pending, escalated, declined, or handed off
  • Time to practical resolution
  • Patient and hospital-staff experience
  • Navigator workload and retention
  • Program cost and resource requirements

Proposed next step

Conduct a workflow-design meeting with hospital transition, social work, nursing, privacy/compliance, and operations representatives to determine whether there is a narrow patient population and discharge point where AfterNest could add value without duplicating existing services.

UCSF Health is the project team’s preferred potential partner for the academic proposal. No partnership, endorsement, or sponsorship is currently confirmed. Real implementation would require hospital, legal, privacy, insurance, clinical, and regulatory review.
