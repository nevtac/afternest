Volunteer with AfterNest

Help someone get from “I know what to do” to “I can actually do it.”

Recovery Navigators provide practical, non-clinical support during the first weeks after hospital discharge. The role is about patience, logistics, consistency, and knowing when to bring the healthcare team back in.

Apply to become a NavigatorSee training standards
Who could be a fit?

Community volunteers and students in pre-health, nursing, public health, social work, medicine, or related fields who can commit to dependable follow-through and clear role boundaries.

What the role looks like

Practical support, one barrier at a time.

A Navigator does not interpret medical instructions or give health advice. The work is to help make approved next steps more usable in the patient’s real life.

Coordinate

Help with transportation logistics, scheduling, caregiver coordination, and approved community resources.

Navigate

Support portals, video visits, digital tools, preferred-language logistics, and finding the right non-clinical resource.

Follow through

Confirm what happened, document the practical task, and escalate anything that moves outside the role.

The most important qualification is reliability.

AfterNest is designed around a simple promise: practical barriers should not disappear after a referral is made. A Navigator stays accountable for the next action until the task is resolved, declined, escalated, or handed off.

Proposed pilot expectations
  • Complete training before patient contact
  • Pass screening required by the operating partner
  • Maintain dependable weekly availability
  • Document practical tasks consistently
  • Escalate clinical or urgent concerns immediately
Training pathway

Prepared before matched.

Every Navigator would complete a structured curriculum and demonstrate understanding of scope, privacy, communication, resources, and escalation.

01

Role & boundaries

Understand what AfterNest can support and what must remain with licensed care.

02

Communication

Practice respectful, patient-centered, culturally aware support and preferred-language logistics.

03

Resources & workflow

Use approved transportation, technology, caregiver, and community-resource pathways.

04

Escalation

Know when to stop, who to contact, and how to document a safe handoff.
