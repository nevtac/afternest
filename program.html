Program model

A practical execution layer between discharge and recovery at home.

AfterNest is designed for patients whose medical discharge plan is in place but whose real-world ability to complete the next steps may still be constrained by transportation, technology, language navigation, caregiver availability, or basic-needs barriers.

Walk through the Recovery Readiness CheckSee Navigator operations
Operating principle

Every priority non-clinical barrier receives a named owner, next action, visible status, and defined close state: resolved, declined, escalated, or formally handed off.

Eligibility

Start with a narrow population and a clear reason for referral.

The proposed pilot should not serve every discharged patient. It should focus on people whose discharge plan may depend on support that is not reliably available at home.

Who

Discharged home without reliable support

Potential starting populations include older adults, people living alone, and patients without a nearby caregiver able to help with practical recovery tasks.

Why

At least one actionable practical barrier

Transportation, technology, language navigation, caregiver logistics, food/basic-needs access, or difficulty connecting to an approved community resource.

How

Partner-approved warm referral

An appropriate care-team member identifies the patient before discharge, explains the optional program, and follows the agreed referral process.

Recovery Readiness Check

Find the practical gap before the patient leaves.

The referral workflow is intentionally limited to what AfterNest needs for non-clinical support. The prototype does not ask for diagnoses, medication lists, symptoms, lab values, or clinical notes.

Readiness questions

Is the next follow-up step known?
Is transportation actually available?
Is a reliable support person available?
Can the patient use required digital tools?
What language is preferred for non-clinical navigation?
Are basic-needs or community-resource barriers present?

Recovery Navigator

One person owns the non-clinical follow-through.

The Navigator is trained, supervised, and accountable for moving approved practical tasks forward while staying inside a clearly defined non-clinical role.

EXECUTE

Move practical tasks forward

Coordinate transportation, support technology use, connect approved resources, help with caregiver logistics, and organize the next practical step.

DOCUMENT

Keep ownership visible

Record the barrier, owner, next action, status, and outcome without collecting unnecessary medical information.

ESCALATE

Know when to stop

Symptoms, medication questions, treatment concerns, urgent issues, or other clinical matters return to the partner-approved healthcare pathway.

Support lifecycle

Approximately 30 days, with resolution as the goal.

The service is time-limited but not calendar-only. A case should close responsibly when priority barriers are resolved, declined, escalated, or transferred to sustainable support.

PRE-DISCHARGE

Warm handoff

Hospital introduces AfterNest and the patient opts into the program.

DAY 0-2

First contact

Navigator confirms communication preferences and priority practical barriers.

WEEK 1

Stabilize logistics

High-priority transportation, technology, language, caregiver, and basic-needs tasks move first.

WEEKS 2-4

Follow through

Open tasks are verified, unresolved items are escalated or handed off, and support is adjusted.

GRADUATION

Close responsibly

Priority barriers have a confirmed outcome or sustainable next owner before closure.

Operating standard

Every task ends in a defined state.

This is the core process discipline behind the model.

Clinical boundary

Designed to complement the care team, not become another clinical service.

AfterNest is not home health, medical case management, qualified medical interpretation, or a replacement for nurses, physicians, social workers, or other licensed care. Its value is in practical execution and visible handoff discipline.
