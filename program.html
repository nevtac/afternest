How AfterNest works

Practical support between the discharge plan and daily life at home.

AfterNest is designed for patients whose medical discharge plan is already in place but who may still lack the transportation, technology, language navigation, caregiver help, or resource access needed to carry it out.

Who the pilot is for

Start with patients who are going home without enough reliable support.

The proposed pilot would focus on a narrow group rather than every discharge. Priority patients could include older adults, people living alone, and people facing multiple access barriers during recovery.

Living support

No reliable person nearby

The discharge plan assumes help is available, but family or caregivers may be distant, unavailable, or unable to manage every practical task.

Access barriers

Real-world obstacles

Transportation, digital access, language navigation, caregiver logistics, or basic-needs barriers make follow-through harder.

Warm referral

Introduced before discharge

An approved hospital team member explains the optional program and connects an eligible patient to AfterNest before they leave.

Recovery Readiness Check

Identify the practical gap without creating another clinical record.

The referral is intentionally limited to information needed for non-clinical support. The academic prototype does not request diagnoses, medication lists, symptoms, lab values, or clinical notes.

Examples of follow-through

What the Navigator can actually help move forward.

The role is deliberately practical. These examples make the proposal more concrete without crossing into diagnosis, treatment, or medical advice.

Transportation

Coordinate the ride

Find an appropriate option, confirm logistics, and verify that transportation is actually arranged.

Technology

Practice the tool

Help the patient use portals, video visits, appointment details, and basic digital workflows.

Navigation

Clarify the next step

Help organize non-clinical logistics and route clinical questions back to the approved healthcare contact.

The patient journey

A defined beginning, middle, and end.

Support is expected to last roughly the first month after discharge, but the goal is responsible resolution rather than simply reaching day 30.

01

Opt in

The patient chooses to participate and the approved practical barriers are identified.

02

Meet the Navigator

A trained person confirms communication preferences and the first practical priorities.

03

Work the plan

The Navigator coordinates, verifies, documents, and escalates as needed.

04

Graduate

Priority barriers are resolved or another sustainable person or service has accepted ownership.

Clear clinical boundary

The hospital owns the medicine. AfterNest owns approved practical follow-through.

Recovery Navigators do not diagnose, treat, interpret symptoms, advise on medications, replace licensed care, or provide emergency response. Clinical concerns return to the partner-approved healthcare pathway.
