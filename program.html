Program design

From discharge plan to practical recovery support.

AfterNest is designed as a hospital-linked, non-clinical recovery support program for patients who may not have enough practical support once they return home.

Core operating principle

Every priority non-clinical barrier gets a named owner, a next action, and a visible status until it is resolved, escalated, declined, or formally handed off.

Eligibility

A deliberately narrow starting point.

The initial pilot should not try to serve every discharged patient. It should focus on people whose discharge plan may depend on support they do not reliably have.

Core population

Older adults, people living alone, and people without a reliable nearby caregiver during the first weeks at home.

Practical barriers

Transportation, technology, language navigation, caregiver access, food/basic needs, and community-resource access.

Hospital fit

Patients identified before discharge by an appropriate care-team member and willing to opt into a non-clinical support program.

The Recovery Readiness Check

Find the practical gaps before the patient leaves.

The referral workflow asks only what AfterNest needs to know to support non-clinical follow-through. The prototype intentionally avoids requesting diagnoses, medications, symptoms, lab values, or detailed medical history.

Readiness questions

Is follow-up logistics understood?
Is transportation available?
Is there a reliable caregiver or support person?
Can the patient use the required technology?
What language does the patient prefer for non-clinical navigation?
Are food, household, or community resources needed?

What happens next

The answers become a personalized Non-Clinical Recovery Support Plan. Each selected barrier is converted into a task for the Recovery Navigator.

Recovery Navigator

One person owns the practical follow-through.

The Navigator is trained, supervised, non-clinical, and accountable for helping the patient move practical tasks forward.

SUPPORT

Help execute

Coordinate transportation, navigate technology, connect community resources, support caregiver logistics, and help organize next steps.

DOCUMENT

Track the task

Record the practical barrier, next action, due date, and resolution status without documenting unnecessary medical information.

ESCALATE

Know the boundary

If the patient raises a clinical question, symptom, medication concern, or treatment issue, route it back to the approved healthcare contact.

Support lifecycle

Approximately 30 days per patient, with resolution as the goal.

PRE-DISCHARGE

Warm handoff

Hospital introduces AfterNest and the patient chooses whether to participate.

DAY 0-2

First contact

Navigator confirms communication preferences and priority practical barriers.

WEEK 1

Stabilize logistics

Transportation, technology, caregiver, language, and basic-needs tasks are moved forward.

WEEKS 2-4

Follow through

Open items are verified, unresolved needs are escalated or handed off, and support is adjusted.

GRADUATION

Close responsibly

Priority barriers are resolved or connected to sustainable support before the case is closed.

Clinical boundary

Designed to complement the care team, not compete with it.

AfterNest is not home health, medical case management, or a substitute for qualified interpretation. The healthcare team retains responsibility for diagnosis, treatment, medication, symptoms, and clinical follow-up.
